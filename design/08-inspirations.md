# 08 — Inspirations: What We Take, What We Leave

Each reference is cited for a specific reason. The standing caveat over all of
them: **their skill floors are far higher than ours may ever be.** We import
feel, not difficulty.

---

## Titanfall 2
**Take:** the overall movement feel — wall-running, grappling, bhopping, double
jump, and above all the "everything chains together" philosophy; hold-jump
autobhop as the proof that generosity and depth coexist; speed preserved through
slides and hops.
**Leave:** the skill ceiling as a barrier — Titanfall's advanced tech
(slide-hop timing chains, wall-kick lurches) is executable here by anyone via
the forgiveness layer; the same-wall re-attach lockout (we allow it,
[02 §3.2](02-movement-mechanics.md)); military-grit presentation.

## Warfork / Warsow (and Echo Point Nova)
**Take:** the momentum-preserving dash, imported nearly whole — a burst that
adds speed and redirects sharply in any direction with zero penalty, building
momentum *through* the turn ([02 §2.6](02-movement-mechanics.md)); Echo Point
Nova's generosity about keeping speed everywhere and its joy of huge open lines.
**Leave:** strafe-jump execution barriers (our air strafing has no mouse-sync
skill check, [02 §2.5](02-movement-mechanics.md)); dash resource scarcity;
arena-FPS hostility to newcomers.

## Ghostrunner
**Take:** clean, flowing parkour; the dash as a course-correction tool
mid-flight; wall-run → slide → grapple chaining as one continuous sentence;
crisp, readable animation poses.
**Leave:** one-hit death and restart pressure (the anti-flow loop); demanding
precision windows; monochrome-grim palette.

## Mirror's Edge / Mirror's Edge Catalyst
**Take:** traversal *flow* as the core fantasy; the single contextual control
that reads intent ([03 §4](03-forgiveness-and-intent.md)); embodied animation —
hands touching the world.
**Leave — emphatically:** the hard momentum reset on bad landings. Named in the
brief as the thing we are the *opposite* of; our landing rules
([01 §2.3](01-core-philosophy.md)) are written directly against it. Also leave:
combat as an awkward interruption (ours never interrupts), first-person
(we're third).

## Sunset Overdrive
**Take:** traversal energy and style — grinding with instant direction changes
(our ziplines), bounce-everywhere verticality (pads, pounds), the game
*celebrating* stylish continuous movement; exuberant animation flourishes.
**Leave:** style meters as pressure (our flourishes are free expression, not
scored obligation); its combat-first structure.

## Team Fortress 2
**Take:** rocket-jumping — the additive-impulse launch, reimagined as the
gun-hand's magic blast; specifically the *practice-mode* convention (no self
damage) promoted to the only rule ([02 §4.3](02-movement-mechanics.md)).
**Leave:** the health tax entirely; the execution knowledge (crouch-timing
subtleties become a simple +25% tuck bonus anyone can hold).

## Overwatch 2
**Take:** the wall-riding variation (hold-to-ride, indefinite at the cap) and
abilities usable while riding; the two-grapple split — pull-to-perch
(consistent, beginner-predictable) vs. swing-for-speed (momentum engine) —
adopted directly as our pull/swing pair ([02 §5.1–5.2](02-movement-mechanics.md)).
**Leave:** cooldown-gated mobility as class identity (movement is everyone's,
always); hero-shooter balance constraints on traversal.

## Gigantic
**Take:** art direction and color wholesale — vibrant, high-saturation,
cel-shaded, candy-bright; painterly stylization for readability, performance,
and agelessness ([06 §1](06-art-direction.md)).
**Leave:** its MOBA structure and character variety burden (one perfect
protagonist instead).

## Deadlock (Valve)
**Take:** character and costume — the full suit + long coat, occult-noir
1930s-meets-steampunk silhouette; confident gun-handling animation layered
over fluid movement ([06 §3–4](06-art-direction.md)).
**Leave:** its palette (Deadlock's muted tones are replaced by Gigantic's
brights); its combat complexity (items, souls, lanes — none of it).

---

## The synthesis, in one sentence

Titanfall's chain, Warfork's dash, Ghostrunner's line, Mirror's Edge's single
contextual verb, Sunset Overdrive's exuberance, TF2's launch, Overwatch's
ride-and-pull, in Gigantic's colors on Deadlock's coat — with every skill floor
sanded down to nothing and every drop of momentum protected by law.
